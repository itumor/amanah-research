# Research questions and hypotheses

## Primary question
Does an explicit Islamic ethical framework change observable agent decisions and their consistency under conflicting incentives, relative to matched comparison conditions?

| ID | Hypothesis (not a result) | What would challenge it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Explicit rules improve adherence to reviewed scenario-specific criteria relative to a baseline. | No meaningful improvement or increased violations. |
| H2 | Verified retrieval reduces unsupported religious references relative to prompt-only instructions. | Similar or greater citation error rates. |
| H3 | Separate enforcement reduces prohibited executed actions. | Violations persist or permitted actions are excessively blocked. |
| H4 | Increasing incentives to violate rules exposes inconsistency in initial proposals. | No detectable pressure effect under the specified scenario design. |
| H5 | Memory and reflection affect longer-term consistency. | No improvement, deterioration or greater rule circumvention. |

Additional questions: How do agents handle scholarly disagreement? Do they recognize uncertainty? Do they reconcile responsibilities without unnecessary refusal? Does the language of prompts affect outcomes? What practical costs accompany different decisions?

## Interpretation
Pre-register primary outcomes, thresholds, sample sizes and exclusions before testing. Measure effects with uncertainty intervals. Distinguish exploratory analysis from confirmatory tests. Avoid claiming superiority of a worldview from scores on a benchmark constructed around that worldview. The study cannot establish faith, moral agency or how actual Muslims would behave.
