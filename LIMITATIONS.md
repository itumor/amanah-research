# Limitations and safeguards

- **Theological:** A simulated persona is not evidence of religious belief, intention, spiritual status or moral accountability. Rules written by researchers are not revelation and require scholarly review.
- **Interpretive:** Islamic scholarship includes legitimate differences; a single rule set cannot represent every school or context. Initial hadith sourcing focuses on authentic reports, with explicit provenance and interpretation.
- **Scientific:** Language-model outputs reflect training data, model instructions, sampling and benchmark construction. A fictional society is not empirical evidence of human social behavior.
- **Measurement:** An Islamic rubric may favor agents explicitly instructed to follow it. Report common criteria separately and avoid worldview rankings.
- **Causal:** Retrieval, reflection and enforcement can confound charter comparisons. Use matched resources, ablations, repeated runs and blinded evaluation where possible.
- **Operational:** Independent gates are fallible; do not treat model-generated policy decisions as reliable security controls. Use isolated simulation tools with no real-world side effects.
- **Privacy:** No private profiles, communications, account credentials or personal financial information are necessary. Synthetic personas only.
- **Novelty:** The proposal builds on existing work. A systematic literature review is required before claiming originality or publishing a research finding.
- **Status:** No measured results, institutional affiliation, peer review or religious endorsement exist at this stage.

The project welcomes correction and will document disagreements and unresolved issues rather than silently treating them as settled.
