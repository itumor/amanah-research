# Source register

Initial reading list, not a systematic literature review. Religious references anchor proposed research topics; their operational interpretation requires scholarly review.

## Academic foundations

1. Bai et al. (2022). *Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073 — principles-based model feedback and training; AMANAH proposes runtime evaluation rather than claiming to reproduce that training method.
2. Park et al. (2023). *Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442 — memory, reflection and planning in simulated agents.
3. *Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Islamic Ethics: Towards Pluralist Ethical Benchmarking for AI* (2023). https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-023-00668-x — relevant existing work on Islamic normative evaluation of AI.
4. *Islamic Ethics and AI: An Evaluation of Existing Approaches to AI using Trusteeship Ethics* (2025). https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-025-00922-4 — relevant discussion of trusteeship and AI ethics.
5. *Islamic virtue-based ethics for artificial intelligence* (2022). https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-022-00028-2 — virtue-based approaches to AI ethics.

## Source governance
Future records should include the original Arabic, translation edition, verse or hadith identifiers, authenticity information where applicable, interpretive context, reviewer, review date, disagreements and version history. Verify source licensing before distributing any bulk text. Do not use an unverified model-generated citation as an authoritative source.
